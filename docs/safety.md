1. Vital Safety Precautions (Pre-Operation)
----------------------------------------------

Operating an industrial-scale sprinkler connected directly to a municipal water system involves massive kinetic energy, immense weight, and high hydrodynamic pressures.

-   Establish a Safe Exclusion Zone: Establish a minimum 200-foot clear radius downwind from the nozzle. A 1.35-inch solid water jet pressurized at 70+ PSI exits with enough force to cause severe physical injury, easily knocking a person down or causing blunt force trauma.
-   Secure the Base Rigidity: Do not attempt to run this device freestanding or on a lightweight tripod. The reactive thrust (recoil) generated by a 1.35-inch stream discharging hundreds of gallons per minute can instantly tip or launch the assembly. Securely anchor the 32-inch sprinkler chassis to a weighted, rigid steel ground platform or stake it heavily into structural footings.
-   Mandatory PPE: Operators must wear high-visibility vests, safety glasses/face shields, and steel-toed boots during setup and activation.
-   Clear the Mechanical Path: Ensure the 2-foot aluminum impact arm is completely clear of hands, tools, and clothing before opening the lines. The return action of a high-tension torsion spring snapping a heavy metal arm back into place can instantly crush fingers.

* * * * *

2. Step-by-Step Hydrant Integration & Opening Procedure
----------------------------------------------------------

To prevent catastrophic system failures, you must bring the system online incrementally. Never open an industrial hydrant line rapidly. Doing so causes a water hammer---a massive hydraulic shockwave that can shatter your printed assembly, rupture hoses, or burst water mains.

Step 1: Pre-Flight Inspection
-----------------------------

1.  Verify that the internal pivot shaft bolt on the mounting hub is lubricated and that the aluminum spoon arm swings freely without catching.
2.  Confirm the nozzle orifice is entirely free of structural debris or 3D-printing artifacts.
3.  Check that the high-tension torsion spring is pinned securely into the balance tail.

Step 2: Line Connection
-----------------------

1.  Thread an industrial 2.5-inch NH/NST fire hose or high-pressure lay-flat line onto the hydrant's side discharge port.
2.  Connect the opposite end securely to the flanged base inlet of your giant sprinkler assembly. Ensure all gaskets are seated perfectly to prevent high-pressure spray leaks at the seams.

Step 3: Initial Line Charging (The Low-Pressure Bleed)
------------------------------------------------------

1.  Attach your hydrant wrench to the top operating nut of the fire hydrant.
2.  Slowly turn the nut counterclockwise only 1 to 2 full rotations. You will hear water begin rushing into the dry line.
3.  Allow the line to fully fill and purge air through the sprinkler nozzle. The water will initially sputter out as air pocketing escapes. Wait until you have a low-pressure, solid stream flowing steadily out of the taper nozzle.

Step 4: Bringing the System to System Operating Pressure
--------------------------------------------------------

1.  Once the air is entirely bled from the system, continue opening the hydrant operating nut slowly (roughly 1 turn every 5 seconds) until the valve is completely back-seated (fully open). This allows the municipal grid to reach its full residual operating pressure safely.
2.  As the fluid velocity hits its target range (60--80 PSI), the kinetic force of the jet hitting the hollow concave scoop will automatically begin driving the oscillating impact cycle, spinning the head to distribute an even blanket of water.

* * * * *

3. System Shutdown Procedure (Closing Down)
----------------------------------------------

1.  Slowly turn the hydrant operating nut clockwise. Spin the wrench gradually to throttle the flow down over a minimum span of 30 to 45 seconds. This slow deceleration safely dissipates the kinetic energy of the moving column of water without triggering a reverse water hammer.
2.  Once the hydrant is fully closed and the sprinkler stops spraying, wait for the standing line pressure to drop to zero.
3.  Open the hydrant's built-in drain valve (or crack a coupling slightly) to bleed off residual static head pressure before disconnecting your lines.
